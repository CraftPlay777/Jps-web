Jps privacy policy

Last updated: November 1, 2026

Jps is an open-source map app created by CraftPlay777. This policy explains what data the app uses and why.

Summary

Jps has no accounts, shows no ads, uses no analytics or trackers, and sends none of your data to any server of the developer (there isn’t one).

Location

The location permission is optional. It is used only on your device to show your position on the map and calculate the distance to a place. Jps only sends it to a server when you ask for a route with Directions (see Third-party services). You can remove the permission in Android settings at any time.

Third-party services

To work, the app connects to these services, which receive your IP address and the data indicated:

  • OpenStreetMap (tile.openstreetmap.org): the default map service; it downloads the map images, so it knows the area you are viewing.
  • Other map services, only if you choose them in Settings: OpenStreetMap France, Humanitarian and CyclOSM (tile.openstreetmap.fr and tile-cyclosm.openstreetmap.fr, run by OpenStreetMap France) or OpenTopoMap (opentopomap.org). The service you choose receives the area you are viewing.
  • 3D globe (only if you open that screen and accept): downloads map images from tile.openstreetmap.org and elevation data from Terrain Tiles, hosted on Amazon Web Services (s3.amazonaws.com). Both receive your IP address. The app adds its name and version to the user agent of the built-in browser that draws the globe, as OpenStreetMap’s rules require.
  • Borders, states and flags (only if you turn those options on and accept): downloads country borders from raw.githubusercontent.com (Natural Earth), state and province limits from github.com (geoBoundaries) and flags from flagcdn.com. These services receive your IP address and the code of the country whose data is requested. The files are saved on your device.
  • Photos and info (only if you leave that option on in Settings and accept): queries Wikipedia (es.wikipedia.org and other Wikipedia languages) for the text and Wikimedia Commons (upload.wikimedia.org) for the photos. Both are run by the Wikimedia Foundation, see your IP address and receive the name or article of the place you search for. Photos are kept only in the phone’s memory while the app is open.
  • Directions (only when you ask for a route and accept): sends the coordinates of your location and the destination to routing.openstreetmap.de (FOSSGIS OSRM service, in Germany), which keeps requests in its logs and sees your IP address. It is the only time Jps sends your location to a server.
  • Nominatim (nominatim.openstreetmap.org): receives the text you search for, the coordinates of a point you mark with a long press or the identifier of the place you choose, to return results, addresses or the outline of an area.

Each service has its own privacy policy and Jps does not control how they apply it. The OpenStreetMap Foundation’s is at osmfoundation.org/wiki/Privacy_Policy.

Jps makes no connection until you accept this policy the first time you open the app.

Data stored on your device

Stored only on your device: your favorites, your last 8 searches, your settings (including language), the last map position you viewed, the cache of map and 3D globe images, and the downloaded border, state and flag files. You can delete the history and favorites in Settings, or delete everything by uninstalling the app or clearing its data from Android.

Sharing

Jps only shares a place when you ask, through Android’s share menu. What is shared is the place name and a link to OpenStreetMap.

Children

Jps does not collect personal data from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, it will be published at github.com/CraftPlay777/Jps and the date above will be updated.

Contact

For questions or problems, open an issue at github.com/CraftPlay777/Jps/issues.
